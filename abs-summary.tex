\begin{EnglishSummary}
With the increasing application of artificial intelligence in healthcare, medical tabular data have become an important source for clinical prediction tasks. Such datasets typically combine heterogeneous patient-related variables, including demographic characteristics, laboratory measurements, and physiological indicators. These variables differ in type, scale and predictive value. The datasets are often small and the classes are frequently imbalanced, which makes learning difficult. Retrieval-based tabular methods address this by using similar training samples during prediction. Recent work such as ModernNCA has shown that the representation in which neighbors are compared strongly affects their performance. This thesis therefore proposes FiTabR, a feature-aware extension of the retrieval-augmented model TabR. FiTabR adds a lightweight attention module before the TabR encoder that generates sample-specific weights for numerical features and applies residual feature modulation, while preserving TabR's retrieval and prediction mechanisms. Comparing metrics of TabR and FiTabR on the Heart Disease dataset, accuracy increased from 0.8841 to 0.8937 and cross-entropy decreased from 0.3586 to 0.3038, although ModernNCA achieved the highest accuracy on this dataset (0.9058). On Pima Diabetes, the accuracy gain was small (0.7529 to 0.7586), and on Stroke Prediction all neural models predicted only the majority class under accuracy-based model selection, with FiTabR showing a slightly lower ROC-AUC than TabR (0.8095 versus 0.8136). On Heart Disease, ablation showed that removing residual preservation reduced accuracy to 0.8647 and uniform weighting increased cross-entropy to 0.3240 compared to the proposed FiTabR model, whereas removing attention scaling did not reduce performance. On the Heart Disease dataset, the attention analysis assigned the highest weights to cholesterol and age, which agrees with cardiovascular domain knowledge, but the lowest weights to exercise-induced ST-segment depression and maximum heart rate, although both are established prognostic indicators. These findings suggest that the benefit of feature-aware representation in retrieval-augmented learning may depend on dataset characteristics such as class balance and missing values, and that the learned attention should be interpreted as model-specific feature emphasis rather than as clinical feature importance.
\end{EnglishSummary}
